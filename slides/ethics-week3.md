A worked example of the display side: an ordinary lecture page with live quiz
results embedded in it. Nothing here is generated by the quiz server — the page
is Markdown, built with pandoc, and the server contributes only the framed
panels.

Build it with:

```bash
pandoc -s -o slides/ethics-week3.html slides/ethics-week3.md
```

Each panel refreshes itself every two seconds. There is no JavaScript on this
page, no websocket, and nothing to configure: the embed URLs name a quiz and a
question, never a session, so this file is written once and runs every year the
unit is taught.

## The trolley problem

A runaway tram is heading towards five people who cannot move. You are standing
beside a lever. Pull it and the tram diverts onto a side track, where it will
kill one person instead.

> Most people say they would pull the lever. Rather fewer say they would push a
> large stranger off a footbridge to stop the tram, though the arithmetic is
> identical.

## What makes a view consequentialist

Consequentialism holds that the rightness of an act depends only on its
outcomes. Two commitments do the work:

1. **Outcomes are what matter.** Nothing else about an act — who performs it,
   what they intend, what they promised — counts except through its effects.
2. **Outcomes are aggregated.** Benefits and harms across different people are
   summed into a single measure.

Rejecting either one takes you out of the family. Deontological views deny the
first; some pluralist views accept the first and deny the second.

## Utility

The aggregated quantity is *utility*. Classical utilitarians identified it with
pleasure minus pain; later writers substitute preference satisfaction, or
wellbeing more broadly. The disagreements matter less than the shared structure.
